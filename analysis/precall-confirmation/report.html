Which confirmation actually gets setter-set calls to show?

Six months of calls point to one habit: the setter texts the lead the day before and gets the calendar invite accepted. The two setters who do this on most of their sets, Dave and Yobani, have the best-showing sets on the team. Texts on the day of the call show no lift, and calls where both the setter and the closer texted on the day showed far worse.

Dave and Yobani text the lead the day before on about 70% of their sets, and almost never text on the day. The other setters text the day before on 5–28% of sets. Quinn texts on the day of half her sets, and her sets show at the team average.

The habit and the setter come together

Within each setter, calls that got a day-before text showed only slightly more than calls that didn't (+1 point, not significant). So the data can't prove the text itself causes the lift. What it does show is that the two setters who make the day-before text routine have sets that show 11 points better.

The day-before text gets invites accepted

When Dave texted the day before, 67% of leads had accepted the invite, against 48% when he didn't. Acceptance is the strongest predictor of showing in the data.

Day-of texts on setter-set calls

One text on the day, from either person, performs about the same as no text. Two texts on the day, one from each, is the worst pattern in the data.

About half of the double texts were replies to a lead who had written first, so some of these leads were already wobbling. Even so, there is no sign that adding a second sender on the day helps.

Proposed workflow for setter-set calls

  1. Setter, day before: the confirmation text (required).Its one job is to get the calendar invite accepted. Name the closer, give the time with the time zone, ask "Still good for [Time] [TZ]?", and offer to resend the invite. This is what Dave and Yobani already do.
  2. Day of: one short text, from one person.The closer sends one line a few hours before: "Hey [Name], [Closer] here. Looking forward to talking at [Time]!" The setter doesn't text on the day, unless the invite still isn't accepted by that morning. Then the setter sends the warm handoff with the Zoom link instead, and the closer skips the line.
  3. Measure it for 4 weeks.Save each text as a Close SMS template so adoption shows in the data. Compare against this six-month baseline, using the same Zoom-attendance method: setter-set show rate 78% (75% in October) and invite acceptance 56%.

How the six-month numbers were measured

  • Calls: every 45-minute meeting on a closer's calendar in Close starting May 1 – Oct 31 (ET), one per lead and start time, including closers who have since left (EJ, Chris, Kameron, Giovana, Jordan W).
  • Showed: the prospect joined the Zoom (anyone other than staff or a notetaker bot), the backstop rule in the decisions log. Checked against the EOD Tracker's lead-level results for Sep–Oct: Zoom agreed on 94% of EOD shows and 99% of EOD no-shows. The EOD Tracker only lists leads from September, so it couldn't be used for May–Aug.
  • Left out of show rates: cancelled meetings (Close doesn't record when a cancel happened, so same-day cancels can't be counted as no-shows); calls where the Zoom room never opened; and VeJohn's calls, which have no Zoom data in Close.
  • Setter-set: in the 21 days before the booking, a setter held a meeting with the lead or had a 2+ minute call with them. Setters: Dave, Jordan K, Gabriel, Quinn, Yobani, Bali, Jose, and Omar for his May–June sets. Dave is David Martin in Close.
  • Texts and calls counted: sent texts, dials and non-sequence emails from the setter or the closer, after booking and before the call, with the same exclusions as the October report (booking welcome texts, bulk blasts, drafts, the last 10 minutes).
  • Within-closer comparisons compare calls on the same closer's calendar and pool the differences, so a closer with a strong lead mix doesn't skew the result.

Does confirming lift show rate?

Somewhat, for day-of confirmation. Closers who confirm on the day tend to have higher October show rates, but the link is moderate and rests on 13 closers, so treat it as a signal worth testing, not proof. Day-before confirmation shows almost no link.

Day-of: moderate positive link

Rank correlation 0.65 across 13 closers (unlikely to be chance, p ≈ 0.02). The straight-line correlation is weaker at 0.42 (p ≈ 0.15), because the three high show-rate closers other than Adam confirm only a quarter to a third of the time.

Day-before: no meaningful link

Rank correlation 0.13, straight-line 0.29. Few closers do it, so there is little to compare.

Confirmers show 9 points higher

Closers who confirm at least a quarter of calls on the day (Adam, Oliver, Tyler, Julien): 74% show rate, 101 of 137. Everyone else: 65%, 369 of 571.

Adam alone doesn't prove it

Adam confirms 86% of calls and shows 72%, above the team's 66% but below Oliver, Julien and Daniel. Daniel shows 74% while confirming only 13%. Lead mix, call volume and ramp status also move show rate.

The link weakens on direct calls

On direct calls only, where the closer is the only one confirming, the rank correlation drops to 0.49 across 12 closers (p ≈ 0.1).

Call by call, confirmed calls don't show more

Direct calls with a day-of touch showed 64% (65 of 102), against 70% (237 of 340) without one. Many day-of texts go to leads who already look shaky, so this comparison is biased against confirming. Direct calls touched the day before did better: 73% (24 of 33) against 64% (148 of 232).

Where reschedule requests arrive

Of 93 lead messages asking to reschedule or cancel, 49 came by text and 16 by other email. 28 were replies to the automated “[Pro] You're confirmed… one last thing” email, which goes out under the closer's name and lands in the closer's inbox. On setter-set calls, 18% of leads also texted the setter back before the closer call, with messages like “Can we do tomorrow same time?”, “Please resend a fresh invite” and “Where is the link?”.

What this means for the setter-set workflow

Nothing in the October messages argues against the setter doing day-before and day-of, plus the closer's day-of one-liner. The six-month data at the top of this page does, though: it points to one day-of text from one person. Whichever version runs, three things have to be true:

  • Whoever confirms gets the replies. If setters confirm, reschedule and “resend the invite” requests come to them. Setters need a way to move the call on the closer's calendar and to resend the invite or add a spouse, or every reply becomes a handoff delay.
  • Closers still get reschedule requests. Leads reply to the automated “You're confirmed” email in the closer's name. Closers need to keep watching that inbox and loop in the setter, or the setter has to see those replies too.
  • Price, program and “can I bring my partner” questions go to the closer. The setter should pass them on rather than answer. One lead wrote “I am scheduled with VeJohn and Jose”, so the templates should say who is who: “I'm Jose, I set up your call; VeJohn is the advisor you'll meet.”

How this was measured

  • Calls: every 45-minute meeting on an active closer's calendar in Close that started between Oct 3 and Nov 2 (ET), one per lead and start time.
  • Touches counted: texts and emails the closer actually sent, and calls they dialed, after the booking and before the call. Times are converted to Eastern.
  • Day before is measured only on calls booked at least 2 days ahead, so there was a real day before to confirm in.
  • Excluded: anything sent within an hour of booking; the standard welcome texts ("Just reading through your app…", "Please accept the calendar invite…", "you'll see a few emails come through…"); the automated pre-call email sequence (counted separately); bulk campaign blasts; drafts and failed sends; and messages in the last 10 minutes before the call ("I'm on the Zoom now").
  • Setter-set vs direct: a call counts as setter-set when, in the 21 days before it was booked, a setter held a meeting with the lead, logged a "Conversation: set to closer" call, or had a phone conversation of 2+ minutes with them. Everything else is direct. Setters covered: Bali, Jose, Jordan K, Yobani, Quinn, Dave and Gabriel.
  • Setter confirmations: setter texts (sent only, bulk blasts removed) and dials to the lead the day before or the day of the closer call, using the same timing rules as the closers. Setter emails were not pulled.
  • Per-call show results: from the EOD Tracker's showed, no-showed and cancelled lead lists, matched on closer, date and Close lead. Same-day cancels count as no-shows.
  • Not visible: texts or calls from personal phones. A closer who confirms outside Close will look like they don't.
  • Assumption: the tracker roster's "Jay Z" is Jordan Zadrozny in Close.
  • All data was read from Close; nothing was changed there.
